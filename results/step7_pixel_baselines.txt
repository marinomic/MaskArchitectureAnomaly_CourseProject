========================================================================
 STEP 7 - PIXEL-BASED ANOMALY SEGMENTATION BASELINES (ERFNet)
 Benchmarks: SMIYC RA-21 | SMIYC RO-21 | FS L&F | FS Static | Road Anomaly
 Metrics   : AuPRC (higher is better), FPR95 (lower is better)
========================================================================

HOW THESE NUMBERS WERE OBTAINED
  Model : ERFNet, the lightweight pixel-based segmentation network, loaded
          from the pretrained Cityscapes weights (erfnet_pretrained.pth).
  Pipeline (see Step7_pixel_baselines.ipynb):
    1. Each validation image is resized to 512x1024 and passed through
       ERFNet once, producing per-pixel logits f of shape (C, H, W).
    2. From the SAME logits, three post-hoc anomaly scores are computed:
         - MSP         : 1 - max_c softmax(f)_c        (low confidence -> anomaly)
         - Max Logit   : - max_c f_c                   (low activation -> anomaly)
         - Max Entropy : - sum_c p_c log p_c           (flat output  -> anomaly)
    3. Ground-truth masks are normalised per dataset to {0: in-distribution,
       1: anomaly}; only images that contain anomaly pixels are scored.
    4. AuPRC and FPR@95TPR are computed from scratch in NumPy (no sklearn),
       pooling pixels across each dataset.

NOTES
  - The ERFNet segmentation mIoU is left as "--": the anomaly pipeline does
    not recompute it, and it does not change across post-hoc methods.
  - Max Logit is the strongest pixel-based score here, consistently beating
    MSP and Max Entropy: raw activations separate anomalies better than the
    normalised softmax confidence on this network.
  - Absolute AuPRC stays low on FS L&F and SMIYC RO-21: small/!far anomalies
    are hard for a pixel-based model with no anomaly-specific training.
